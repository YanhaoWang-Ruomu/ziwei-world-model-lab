Living three-scene update (September 2026):
The first two plates now add independently advected cloud planes, animated crane
sprites, bounded water refraction following the painted river and changing light.
The final gorge shares a sampled ridge contour across mountain haze and star
extinction. These are browser-rendered artistic animations, not recorded footage.
Ambient wind, water, birds, insects and chimes are procedurally synthesized with
Web Audio. No audio recording is sampled or downloaded, and no paid API is used.
Sound starts only after an explicit click, has independent volume/mute controls,
and fades out when the viewer leaves the scene or hides the page.
https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices

Ridge integration refinement:
The alpha-derived crest and a separately smoothed horizon drive the same air
layer above and below the distant peaks. Near ridges retain their photographic
detail; the far horizon loses contrast and stellar visibility through simulated
air haze. Fog is an artistic depth cue, not a meteorological measurement.

Celestial lighting refinement (September 2026):
moon-lro-2025.jpg: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit, Ernie Wright.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_2k.jpg
2025 LROC color mosaic, 2048 x 1024, centred on lunar longitude zero.
Runtime spherical projection includes calculated lunar phase, north-pole
orientation and geocentric optical libration from Astronomy Engine. Surface
relief is represented by albedo detail, not a terrain displacement simulation.

milkyway-gaia.jpg: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio,
Deep Star Maps 2020, Ernie Wright. J2000 celestial plate carree projection.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004800/a004851/milkyway_2020_4k.exr
4096 x 2048 linear EXR converted to standard sRGB JPEG for browser delivery.
This background is rendered from star catalogues, not a camera photograph.
It omits the bright Hipparcos and Tycho stars; selectable foreground stars
remain sourced from HYG. RA zero is at the centre and increases to the left.
Both scenic skies transform the map with the same reference frame as the stars.
NASA imagery use guidance: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

Solar limb darkening, fine surface granulation, optical bloom, faint rays,
lunar earthshine and Earth's atmospheric edge are aesthetic simulations.
Bodies remain enlarged for legibility. Exposure is composed for simultaneous
readability of stars, terrain and Earth, not a single calibrated camera exposure.
Stellar scintillation is used in the mountain scene and suppressed in the Earth
composition. Reduced-motion and pause settings also stop the stellar shimmer.
No affiliation with or endorsement by NASA is implied.

Solar and lunar ground illumination share the ephemeris directions. The globe
uses geocentric Moon direction and its illuminated fraction, with artistic night
exposure, ocean glints and directional cloud-shadow offsets. Landscape relief
is inferred only for subtle image relighting; it is not reconstructed 3D terrain.
Solar surface and coronal streamers are procedural artwork, not current solar
observations. Water highlights and cloud tint use the same projected light sources.

Celestial encounters (2026-09 update):
Fireballs use a fragmented luminous head and a slowly fading atmospheric train.
Meteor-shower tracks share a radiant. Comets have a curved dust tail and a
straighter blue ion tail. These are amplified process illustrations, not
current observations or forecasts. Meteor/fireball/shower effects belong to
the atmospheric sky; the Earth composition is an artistic composite view.
The lunar halo is projected from a 22-degree cone around the Moon, with a
reddish inner edge and diffuse outer edge (ice-crystal refraction).
https://www.weather.gov/lub/events-2008-20080122_halo
https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter1-3/

Planet parade uses ephemeris coordinates at 2022-06-24 21:00 UTC, corresponding
to 2022-06-25 05:00 UTC+8 at the scene's reference location. Connecting guides
show apparent positions, not planets lined up in three-dimensional space.
https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/planetary-alignments-and-planet-parades/

Solar and lunar eclipses are explicit 38-second process demonstrations, with
enlarged disks. Their occultation geometry is illustrative, not a date/location
eclipse solver. The Earth shadow path is illustrative. The rest of the sky uses
the displayed ephemeris; return-to-now exits the demonstration.
https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/geometry/
https://science.nasa.gov/moon/eclipses/
